# Product and UX Quality Standard

For user-facing features, design the full workflow rather than a static screen.

Consider, where relevant:
- User role, goal, context, frequency, and cost of error.
- Information hierarchy, navigation, terminology, and discoverability.
- Empty, loading, success, partial success, error, disabled, and permission states.
- Validation at the correct boundary; actionable and accessible error messages.
- Responsive layouts, keyboard use, focus management, semantic controls, and contrast.
- Confirmation for destructive or irreversible actions; avoid unnecessary confirmations for reversible low-risk actions.
- Audit trail and status visibility for business-critical actions.
- Reuse of the project's established design system; do not introduce a competing visual language without reason.

Use current official design systems and established products as evidence, not templates to copy blindly. Evaluate product fit, accessibility, maintainability, and actual user tasks.
